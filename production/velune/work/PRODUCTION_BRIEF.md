# VELUNE
## Wonder within. — Premium filled chocolate / 15-second production brief

**Version 1.0 · Proposed fictional campaign · Prepared 2026-09-09**

The project is an original chocolate adaptation of the supplied reference's choreography. It preserves the 12-shot structure, 11 hard cuts, question-mark transformation, macro observer movement, laboratory pullback, three-second printed report and living final hold. It does not reuse the source's product, identities, labels, commercial offer, dialogue, music or effects.

**Status:** this is a preproduction package. Editable concept artwork, detailed prompts, exact timelines and a Blender blockout generator have been created. ## 1. The creative idea

A small chocolate contains a world of discovery. We start inside an oversized chocolate landscape, show three inviting flavours, turn curiosity itself into chocolate, inspect the centre, reveal the people inventing it, then give the product and fictional report time to read.

**VELUNE**, pronounced **veh-LOON**, is the working fictional brand. **The Filled Collection** is its product line. **The Centre Report** is the fictional editorial publication. **Wonder within.** is the campaign line. The wording connects the question-mark sequence with a literal filled centre rather than adding an unrelated luxury slogan. These names are creative proposals, not claims of trademark clearance or existing businesses.

The film should feel playful enough to justify the impossible food scale but restrained enough to read as premium chocolate. Use dark cocoa, plum and cream as the stable world; use the three filling colours as controlled accents. Avoid random gold dust, perpetual splashes, floating ingredients and extra camera moves.

### Product range and continuity

Proposed normal-size dimensions are 30 × 30 × 18 mm, with approximately 1.5 mm of shell; these establish a modelling starting point, not a manufacturing specification. The sleeve/carton starts at 120 × 40 × 180 mm and carries “06 FILLED CHOCOLATES.” Macro products and laboratory specimens are deliberately enlarged instances; do not infer edible scale from the human set.

The provided package fronts and report are flat vector concepts. Their chocolate drawings are placeholders for design review, not realistic hero imagery. Replace those illustrations with approved product renders before a final campaign render. This is not a print-production dieline and contains no nutrition, ingredient, allergy, country-of-origin or other regulated retail label information. No real-world product or commercial claim is implied.

## 4. Shot-by-shot production direction

### S01 — Curiosity through chocolate
**0.000000–1.750000s · n000–n041 · 42 frames**

The near chocolate rim fills the edges. Behind it, separate folded rings narrow toward an opening around the host's face. A plum background sits farther back. Avoid a flat doughnut, smooth CGI portal or a tunnel whose texture repeats visibly. Thin caramel veins may catch highlights, but they must not read as gold wiring.

Begin with a curious neutral gaze. At n14 the eyes are slightly upward and the head has begun a gentle tilt. By n41 there is a subtle open smile. The camera stays effectively locked. Face scale and aperture registration are more important than adding motion. Do not animate eating in the opening.

The opening title is a screen-space overlay, not text stamped into the tunnel: small VELUNE above The Centre Report, cream, upper-left safe area. The supplied transparent overlay provides a design starting point. Choose the final font/spacing as original branding; there is no need to imitate the former proprietary title.

Use a broad warm upper/front-left key and enough neutral fill to keep eyes readable. Foreground chocolate can be softer than the face, but its silhouette must stay legible. Block without depth of field first. Key poses: P01 n0, P02 n14, P03 n41.

### S02 — Pistachio box
**1.750000–2.125000s · n042–n050 · 9 frames**

The carton is centred on the same satin plum cylinder used across the three-product montage. A narrow brushed-gold edge may define the support; it should not sparkle or emit light. The carton contact point never leaves the pedestal.

The first and last orientations are proposed -9° and +9° yaw. This is a short excerpt of a rigid turn, not a complete 360° spin. Use nearly linear interpolation. Every corner, surface graphic and printed word moves with the same rigid root. The front image must be a deterministic texture, not AI-generated lettering. Keep enough front face exposed to identify the variant.

One nine-frame view is too short to read a paragraph. Prioritise brand, colour and product silhouette. Do not add a floating flavour caption or a packaging-opening animation. P04 fixes the initial product silhouette and registration.

### S03 — Raspberry box
**2.125000–2.500000s · n051–n059 · 9 frames**

Hard-replace the pistachio carton with raspberry at n51. Reuse the same mesh dimensions, camera, pedestal height, lighting and pivot. The colour and variety name change, not the scale or style of the box.

Preserve the same -9° to +9° yaw profile for this proposed version. If a later render uses a different front angle, record it as a deliberate revision rather than compensating by moving the camera. No crossfade and no green-to-red morph. P05 fixes the replacement frame.

### S04 — Caramel box
**2.500000–2.875000s · n060–n068 · 9 frames**

Hard-replace raspberry with caramel at n60, again at exactly the same contact and screen placement. The amber face should remain distinct from the shell's brown and the gold pedestal edge.

The cut to the transformation comes immediately after n68. No carton needs to fly out or shatter to motivate it. P06 fixes the replacement; inspect the n68/n69 cut adjacency in the edit. These three match-cut products should feel like one disciplined family.

### S05 — The question becomes chocolate
**2.875000–4.708333s · n069–n112 · 44 frames**

This is the film's main controlled deformation. The action plane is pale pistachio rather than the source turquoise. A tasting fork and a small part of the hand remain low-centre, replacing the waffle cone. The empty upper field establishes where the question will appear.

Keep the fork empty through n78. Bonbons appear one per frame from n79 through n90. Place them from the lower stem upward and around the hook. The proposed twelve screen anchors inherit the reference silhouette; use them as hand-fit projection targets, not surveyed positions. At n91–92 a viewer must be able to read the question-mark form before it collapses.

At n93 start adjacent surface bridges. By n94 these are clearly visible; by n100 the open area is largely filled. At n112 the result is a thick irregular spread with broad curved highlights, a few raised remnants and no explosive splash. The fork stays registered. The melt is intentionally surreal in speed; do not invent heat flames, steam or a hotplate.

Use a baked deformation or equal-topology shape-key sequence whose key poses are approved. The provided Python loft is only a surface-motion envelope and can self-overlap: refine it, remove any intersections and create the appetising final surface before generation. Unconstrained fluid physics should not choose the silhouette or finish time.

No gravity-driven rain of bonbons, big collisions or chaotic bounce. The key visual change is that readable negative space becomes a rich continuous material. Keep the projected lower-right shadow direction coherent as surfaces merge. Key poses P07–P14 include empty, first appearance, partial hook, full question, bridges, filled opening and settled spread.

### S06 — The centre reveal
**4.708333–5.833333s · n113–n139 · 27 frames**

A single already-opened pistachio chocolate half dominates the centre, supported by an upright stainless tasting fork. The cut face is oriented to camera. The shell is thin and brittle-looking; the centre is dense and softly granular, not luminous. The product does not newly split in this shot.

Keep the fork handle fixed in the image. Observer A starts lower-left and B upper-right at n113. By n126 they are approximately left/right. By n139 A is upper-left and B lower-right. Put separate head/shoulder rigs on controlled background arcs and adjust their gaze; do not roll the camera and rotate the support with them.

The two people must remain distinct, with consistent wardrobe and no duplicate faces. Their faces can be soft relative to the chocolate but not so blurred that their changing arrangement disappears. Warm skin reflections and narrow cream cards may appear on the metal. Avoid a chrome fork that bends as if it were liquid. P15, P16 and P17 are the three required relative-motion checks.

### S07 — The discovery studio
**5.833333–7.916667s · n140–n189 · 50 frames**

Start with two specimens on a small rectangular plum plinth: three whole chocolates stacked on the left and two on the right. Each bonbon is the same approved shape. These are enlarged display specimens; the scene is deliberately playful rather than a literal factory tour. The left stack stays taller at every frame.

The close view holds through n163. From n164 to n189, move the camera backward in real 3D space. As the viewpoint retreats, two back-facing chocolatiers and a tasting console enter the foreground. The products remain fixed in world space. This changing foreground/product relationship is the shot's reveal; scaling down a flat image does not reproduce it.

The proposed local path is supplied numerically in lab_camera_route.json. It starts at (0,-2.5,1.65), ends at (0,-10,1.9), uses a 50 mm lens and shifts its aim from z1.45 to z1.20. These are starting coordinates. Fit the amount of foreground occlusion and final projected product size before approval. Prevent the camera from passing through a head, lamp, console or set wall.

Use a single soft overhead product emphasis with dim fill, two side lamps and restrained ivory jackets. No glowing laboratory liquids or fictional scientific efficacy claims. A little haze is optional only if needed for a defined light beam; general fog is not part of the concept.

The camera uses monotone smoothstep acceleration and deceleration. The performers make small shoulder/arm movements and do not turn to address the viewer. No internal cut and no lens zoom. P18–P22 check close hold, pullback onset, midpoint occlusion and wide landing.

### S08 — Raspberry
**7.916667–8.625000s · n190–n206 · 17 frames**

A raspberry is the only ingredient on the stage. It needs clustered drupelets, tiny soft hairs, natural asymmetry and a matte-to-satin response, not the continuous seeded skin of a strawberry. There is no green strawberry calyx.

Use the same plum pedestal vocabulary as the carton montage with a composition appropriate to the smaller object. Keep generous upper negative space. The raspberry turns rigidly -7° to +7° as a proposed starting curve; no falling entry, rolling collision or loose fruit particles. P23 establishes the ingredient and contact.

### S09 — Pistachio
**8.625000–9.291667s · n207–n222 · 16 frames**

At n207, hard-replace the raspberry with an already-open pistachio shell/kernel assembly. The cream-beige shell, dry edge and muted green nut should read as natural ingredient textures. Keep both shell halves and kernel in one stable group so they do not drift apart during the turn.

This is not a shot of the shell opening. Do not spend the sixteen frames on a pop or explosion, and do not add a peanut. Repeat the same camera and support. P24 fixes the silhouette and replacement timing.

### S10 — Filled chocolate serving
**9.291667–10.083333s · n223–n241 · 19 frames**

At n223, hard-replace the nut with a low cream dish carrying one whole chocolate behind one opened caramel half. The cut surface faces forward and shows a consistent shell boundary. The caramel is contained, with a subtle rounded meniscus. There is no extra flowing caramel ribbon; the earlier transformation already supplies the large material event.

Rotate the whole dish/chocolate arrangement together. Maintain all contact points and the exact count of two chocolate pieces. Finish the turn by n241 and cut cleanly to the printed report. P25 and P26 establish entry and pre-cut orientation.

### S11 — The Centre Report
**10.083333–13.083333s · n242–n313 · 72 frames**

The card is one printed surface for the entire 72-frame interval. Its headline, three pictured cartons, badge, footer and product art must move together. It is not a live arrangement of three freestanding packages in front of a plum wall.

The new layout contains VELUNE, The Centre Report, the three flavours, a circular “03 CENTRES” badge, The Filled Collection and Edition 01. The circular badge replaces the former retail price without inventing a sale. No retailer logos are carried over.

Allow only a tiny initial rock, approximately 1.1° as a starting choice, settling by n254. The rest is a protected reading interval. Do not add a page turn, sliding products, animated headline or call-to-action banner. Preserve artwork aspect ratio and active-image safe margins. P27 is the entry; P28 is the clean hold.

The supplied PNG/SVG is editable concept artwork with flat chocolate illustrations. Replace those illustrations with approved renders, but keep final lettering deterministic. The strongest production choice is to bypass Seedance for this shot entirely and insert the rendered/comped card directly.

### S12 — Wonder within
**13.083333–15.000000s · n314–n359 · 46 frames**

Cut to a separate wider tunnel camera at n314. Reuse host A and the exact chocolate-fold asset so this feels like returning to the same world. The fork is already at the tasting position. A small withdrawal, subtle mouth/eye response and restrained smile are sufficient.

The host is much smaller than in the opening. Several receding chocolate layers provide scale and depth. Reserve the right side of the central aperture for “Wonder within.” and keep that text stationary from n314 through the final picture n359. Do not display the opening report title again.

This is a living composition hold, not a repeated frozen picture. No final push-in, fade to black, extra product cut, new tagline animation or sixteenth second for a music tail. P29 and P30 must share the same framing and identity.

## 5. Camera, light and material controls

All values below are editable implementation choices. They are not measurements of the source production.

| Setup | Starting lens/projection | Movement | Focus priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close tunnel | 50 mm perspective | Locked | Host face; readable near-food contour |
| Cartons | 75 mm perspective | Locked | Brand and carton edges |
| Question/spread | Orthographic, width 5.5 local units | Locked | Full silhouette and hand/fork registration |
| Macro centre | 85 mm perspective | Locked camera; background rigs move | Cut surface and upright support |
| Studio | 50 mm perspective | Backward route n164–189 | Product initially; reveal layers readable |
| Ingredients | 75 mm perspective | Locked | Natural textures and contact |
| Report | Orthographic front-facing | Card settles, not camera | Exact typography |
| Wide tunnel | 30 mm perspective | Locked | Stable host identity and tagline space |

For the studio route, `t = clamp((n−164)/25, 0, 1)` and `u = 3t²−2t³`. Then `camera = (0, −2.5−7.5u, 1.65+.25u)` and `target = (0, 0, 1.45−.25u)`. Before n164, u=0. The checked numerical route is monotone and ends at n189. It must still be rendered to assess occlusion, perspective and collision.

Start shell roughness around .22–.34, caramel around .16–.25, dense praline/ganache around .28–.50 and satin packaging around .45–.60. These are artistic starting points, not food measurements. Use sparse micro-bump with asset-scale detail rather than exaggerated pitting. Broken shell edges need geometric irregularity; broad faces need cleaner reflections. Foil should reflect a shaped source, not glow.

Use broad warm upper-left keys, weaker neutral fills and controlled cream reflection cards. Keep dark chocolate readable without washing it grey. The macro set can be darker and more intimate; the ingredient stage can be brighter. Do not change the product's base colour to compensate for inconsistent lighting. Use the same colour-management transform for comparison renders.

The blockout script disables depth of field and motion blur. Add those after camera approval. A suggested final shutter is 0.25–0.5 frame, with reduced blur on fast label turns. This is a look choice, not an observed shutter measurement. Never allow blur to interpolate across a hard cut. Delivery can be 1080p even when a lower-resolution generated appearance plate is used; that does not turn the plate into native 1080p detail.

### Music structure

Use one original score with three longer sections. Suggested tempo: 80 BPM in 4/4. This is not an estimate of the source audio. At that tempo a half beat is 0.375 seconds, matching each carton's duration, but the overall edit is not entirely beat-aligned. Do not shift cuts to make it so.

| Musical section | API duration | Character |
|---|---:|---|
| Curiosity and assembly | 4,708 ms | Sparse warm motif, light pulse, gentle lift into the question mark |
| Centre and discovery studio | 5,375 ms | Same theme with a little harmonic expansion; clean ingredient punctuation |
| Report reading and resolve | 4,917 ms | Thinner arrangement, room for voice and reading, short ending |

The three durations total 15,000 ms and each exceeds the documented 3,000 ms minimum for a music-plan section [4]. Their millisecond rounding is not the edit's precision authority: visual cut n113 is 4.708333 seconds, and all sound accents must be placed against the frame/sample grid in post.

Choose soft marimba or felt piano, muted pizzicato and restrained warm bass. Avoid a busy lead, vocals, choir, epic trailer drums and large drops. Compose the final decay to finish inside the 15 seconds. `force_instrumental` is a prompt-mode field in the inspected schema, so the composition-plan payload instead uses empty lyric lines and negative vocal styles. Listen and reject any unwanted singing [4].

### Sound-effect cue design

Ten separate effect prompts are in studio/sfx_cues.json and the audio cue CSV. FX02 supplies optional package ticks at n42/51/60. FX03 supplies selected small appearance ticks at n79/82/85/88/90; sounding all twelve can become comic. FX04 adds dense folding movement at n93–112. FX06 follows the backward reveal at n164–189. FX08 is the brief paper movement at n242–253.

A chocolate snap at the macro cut is an optional stylised accent, not a claim that the chocolate visibly fractures there. Similarly, ingredient clicks are editorial punctuation rather than invented impacts. The source-like turntable shots do not show products being dropped. The final taste effect is optional and should be omitted if the approved animation does not visibly support contact. Avoid amplified chewing and saliva.

The SFX endpoint's minimum generated duration is 0.5 seconds [5]. Generate isolated assets with short attacks and clean tails, then trim them to the 24 fps grid. Do not expect the generator to obey a 0.1-second effect request. Sound generation and sound placement are different steps.

### Mix and silence

Start music ducking around 4–7 dB beneath narration and adjust by ear. Use short smooth fades so the background does not pump. Keep SFX below the speech except for a deliberately isolated accent. Reserve the studio reveal for the visual joke; do not narrate every ingredient. A short low-level final tail is enough—no abrupt amputation and no extension past 15 seconds.

Deliver separate voice, music and SFX stems at 48 kHz, plus a mixed PCM WAV of 720,000 samples. Compressed formats may have encoder padding, so verify the decoded sample count rather than relying solely on a player's rounded duration. The conform helper writes a PCM-audio MOV master; generate any delivery MP4 separately after checking the master.
